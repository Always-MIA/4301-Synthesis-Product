# Support & Well-Being

## Support Is Part of the Picture

Understanding the challenges of aviation work is only one part of the conversation.

The other part is knowing what support can look like.

Support for aviation workers can come from different places, including coworkers, employers, unions, professional mental-health providers, and personal relationships.

There is no single resource that works for everyone, and what is available can depend on an individual's airline, location, employment situation, and personal circumstances.

## Peer Support

Sometimes the person who understands the experience best is another person who has experienced it.

Peer-support programs can give aviation workers an opportunity to talk with someone who understands the work environment and the situations that come with it.

Peer support is not necessarily a replacement for professional mental-health care. Instead, it can provide another layer of support and a place to start.

## Employee Assistance Programs

Many employers offer Employee Assistance Programs, commonly known as EAPs.

Depending on the employer, EAP services may provide confidential assistance related to mental health, stress, personal concerns, or other issues.

Because EAP programs differ between employers, employees should check their own company's available resources to understand what services are offered.

## Critical-Incident Support

Some aviation organizations provide resources specifically for employees who experience serious or potentially traumatic events.

These programs may provide opportunities to speak with trained peers or professionals after a critical incident.

Availability and eligibility can vary depending on the airline, union, organization, and type of incident.

## Professional Mental-Health Support

Professional support can include therapists, counselors, psychologists, psychiatrists, or other qualified mental-health professionals.

Seeking professional support does not mean that someone has failed to handle their job or that their experience is unusual. Mental-health care can be one part of maintaining overall well-being.

## Industry Resources

Aviation organizations and flight-attendant organizations may also provide educational materials, support programs, and information about available resources.

However, resources are not universal.

A program offered through one airline, union, or organization may not be available to someone working somewhere else. Always check whether a resource applies to your specific employment situation.

## Building a Culture of Well-Being

Support should not only appear after someone reaches a breaking point.

A healthier approach to workplace well-being includes making resources easier to find, reducing the stigma around asking for help, encouraging conversations about fatigue and stress, and recognizing that mental health is part of overall employee well-being.

### Key Takeaway

**Support can come from multiple places, and knowing where to look is an important first step.**

You do not have to wait until a situation becomes overwhelming before learning what resources are available.
